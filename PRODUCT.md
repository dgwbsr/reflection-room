# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Home studio owners and DIY listeners planning acoustic treatment in a spare room, home office, or converted bedroom. They work at a desk with a laptop, often in normal daylight, and want a defensible panel layout without learning acoustic modelling software. The job: place a speaker and a listening position, see where the first reflections land on the four walls, and leave with a plan they can install.

## Product Purpose

A single-screen plan-view calculator for first-order reflection points. It turns room dimensions, source position, and listener position into wall locations, path lengths, incidence angles, and arrival delays relative to the direct sound. Success is a layout a user can mark on the wall and hand to a helper, exported as SVG or printed as a PDF.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, practical. Plain language over jargon, exact numbers over false confidence, and a drafting-table feel rather than studio-gear theatre. The interface should feel like a well-made measuring tool: quiet until you need it, legible at a glance, and trustworthy when the numbers matter.

## Anti-references

- Dark neon "pro audio" interfaces with glowing meters and fake spectrum displays.
- Photoreal 3D room simulators that hide the geometry the user needs to check.
- Generic SaaS dashboards with oversized hero metrics and card grids.
- Acoustic-brand marketing sites that prioritise mood photography over measurement.
- Overstated precision: showing four decimal places or claiming a panel guarantees a result.

## Design Principles

1. Geometry first. Every number has a visible ray, point, or dimension behind it.
2. Show the work. The reflection path and the mirror method are part of the answer, not hidden in a black box.
3. One screen, no wizard. Room, placement, and results stay in view together.
4. Forgiving input. Drag for intuition, type for precision, and never lose a valid layout.
5. Output that travels. The plan should survive export, print, and a trip to the hardware store.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Keyboard-operable markers with arrow-key nudging and visible focus. Colour is never the only signal: source, listener, reflection point, and panel zones use distinct shapes and labels. Reduced-motion support. Touch targets at least 44 px. Numeric fields have visible labels and units. Text and data meet 4.5:1 contrast on their surfaces.
